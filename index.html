THE RESEARCH THREAD

From molecular insight
to scientific software.

I am a PhD researcher in computational protein biophysics, machine learning and quantum-computing modelling, nearing completion of my doctoral thesis. My work connects protein dynamics, molecular simulation and scientific computing.

Before my PhD, I built full-stack applications for cheminformatics, bioinformatics and production ML. My aim is to bring AI and quantum computing together in hybrid drug-discovery pipelines that support better decisions in molecular design, simulation and optimisation.

01QUANTUM COMPUTING

Protein modelling for molecular state analysis

A research framework representing proteins through residue-level physical features, interaction structure and sampled state distributions.

Exploring how quantum circuit sampling and thermodynamic reasoning can offer interpretable views of protein energy landscapes and residue interaction networks.

Quantum samplingProtein energeticsResidue networks

02SCIENTIFIC COMPUTING

Solvation-aware protein analysis with JAX

A high-performance Python library for protein solvation analysis, using analytical and numerical solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) algorithms with parallel optimisation enabled by JAX.

JAXSASAHPC
Research focus +

Studying how solvation-aware molecular features can improve the interpretation of protein conformational ensembles and molecular dynamics trajectories.

The related manuscript, JaxProtSASA, is listed as in press.

03GEOMETRIC MACHINE LEARNING

Learning protein conformational ensembles

GNN- and transformer-based manifold learning, with residue-level and atom-level graph representations for analysing protein conformational flexibility and ensemble behaviour.

PyTorch GeometricMDAnalysisTransformers
Research focus +

Working with molecular dynamics-derived data structures using Python, PyTorch, PyTorch Geometric, NumPy, SciPy and MDAnalysis.

Presented research talks at Evotec on protein conformational ensembles and graph-based ML representations.

Ongoing research is described at an abstract level. Further technical details are available for discussion.
